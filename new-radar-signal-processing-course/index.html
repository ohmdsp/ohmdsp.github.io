New Radar Signal Processing Course

Building a modern, hands-on radar curriculum at USU


Jul 3, 2026 Radar Signal Processing DSP Teaching

I am developing a new Radar Signal Processing course at Utah State University (USU) for Fall 2026 semester. The course will be ECE 5930/6930 and enrollment is already fill with a mix of graduate students and senior undergraduate students.

This course is designed to develop a strong understanding of first principals and then bridge theory and practice so students can start to do real signal-processing with real noisy radar data.

Why this course?

Radar sits at the intersection of electromagnetics, statistical signal processing, detection and estimation, and RF engineering. Many students encounter pieces of this puzzle in separate classes, but rarely see how the full radar processing picture fits together. The goal of this course is to connect those pieces in a way that is both rigorous and practical.

What students will learn

The course will focus on core ideas and implementation-oriented skills, including:

  1. Radar fundamentals and system-level thinking
  2. Pulse compression and matched filtering
  3. Range-Doppler processing and clutter considerations
  4. Detection concepts (CFAR and thresholding strategies)
  5. Angle estimation basics and array processing intuition
  6. Introductory tracking and state estimation concepts
  7. Imaging radar fundementals
  8. Introduction to Space-Time Adaptive Processing (STAP)

We will also discuss where machine learning can help, and where classical signal processing remains the best tool.

Hands-on philosophy

A key design objective is to make this a build-and-test course, not just a lecture course. Students will work through guided labs and mini-projects where they:

  1. Simulate radar waveforms in software
  2. Implement collection and processing using hardware and software
  3. Evaluate performance with both ideal and real-world noisy radar data

The intention is for students to leave with both conceptual understanding and practical confidence needed for industry or research.

Course development process

Designing the course has involved balancing depth, pacing, and prerequisites. I am structuring content so that students with a course in discrete-time signals and systems can move quickly into radar-specific methods while still reinforcing signal processing first-principles.

I am also prioritizing clear visualizations and reproducible computational examples so difficult ideas become easier to internalize. Concepts will be reinforced through individual projects on labs.

Looking ahead

I will share updates as the course continues to develop, including sample topics, lab ideas, and lessons learned from teaching the first offering.
