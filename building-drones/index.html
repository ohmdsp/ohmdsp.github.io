Building Drones

A journal of builds, payloads, and lessons learned


Mar 1, 2026 Drones Payloads Engineering RF DSP Teaching

Table of Contents

Over the past two years, I have been building drones and learning through every success, failure, redesign, and field test. This post starts a running journal of those experiences.

I will keep updating this page with images and stories from drones that are already flying, platforms that are currently under construction, and concepts that are still in the prototype phase.

Why I am documenting this

Building drones is one of the best ways to connect theory with reality. Airframes, power systems, RF links, controls, sensing, and software all have to work together under real constraints.

For me, this work is not only about assembling hardware. It is about system-level engineering, practical tradeoffs, and learning how to move from an idea to something that can actually fly and do useful work.

What this journal will include

As this series grows, I will add:

  1. Build logs for different drone platforms
  2. Integration notes for payloads and onboard systems
  3. Design decisions and tradeoff discussions
  4. Test results, failures, fixes, and lessons learned
  5. Photos from bench work, integration, and flight testing

Connection to intern programs and payload development

This drone work connects directly to the intern programs I have developed. Many of the same themes show up repeatedly:

  1. Start with a clear mission vision
  2. Break the system into practical subsystems
  3. Iterate quickly with test-and-learn cycles
  4. Use first principles when troubleshooting

I will also tie these posts to drone payloads I have worked on or am actively developing, including sensing, communications, and signal-processing-oriented payload concepts.

Looking ahead

I plan to keep this as a living post and link to deeper technical writeups as they are published.

If you are working on similar drone builds, especially projects that blend flight hardware with RF or DSP payloads, I would be glad to compare notes.
