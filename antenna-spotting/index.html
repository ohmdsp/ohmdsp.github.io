Antenna Spotting

A communications antenna field journal with ECE5660 students


Apr 18, 2026 Antennas Communications ECE5660 Teaching RF

One of my new hobbies is what I call antenna spotting: walking around and identifying real communications antennas in the wild.

This semester, I took students from my ECE5660 class (Communications Theory I) outside to walk around the campus area and spot, classify, and discuss antennas in real deployment settings. I also assigned a project for students to do the same thing around town.

The response was excellent. Students found the activity practical, memorable, and much more informative than seeing antennas only in slides.

Why this has been so useful

Antenna theory can feel abstract until you see hardware mounted on real buildings, poles, towers, and infrastructure. Once students start connecting radiation concepts, polarization, frequency bands, and deployment constraints to physical systems, the course material becomes much more intuitive.

In these walks and projects, students practiced asking questions like:

  1. What service might this antenna support?
  2. What frequency range is likely being used?
  3. What clues suggest omnidirectional versus directional behavior?
  4. What tradeoffs were likely made in placement and mounting?

Class activity and project approach

The campus walk gave students a guided starting point with immediate discussion. The follow-on project, where I asked them to go find at least seven antennas in the wild around town, pushed them to observe independently and reason from real evidence.

Typical outcomes included:

  1. Better recognition of common antenna form factors
  2. Stronger system-level thinking about technologies and coverage
  3. More confidence connecting theory to engineering practice
