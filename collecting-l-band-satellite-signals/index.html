Collecting L-band Satellite Signals

My first attempt at collecting GOES-18 downlink


Nov 29, 2025 RF Antenna Satellite

In the past, I have captured signals at 137.1 MHz from the NOAA 19 weather satellite using a discone antenna without any pointing. It was exciting to see weather images appear on the computer monitor after processing the signals. At the time, I planned to find a way to set up a tracking capability so I could track the satellites while pointing a higher gain antenna at them to get better signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) and longer collection time. Well, I have finally put together a system that can do this for L-band and UHF frequencies. A system adequate for x-band is in the works.

First Collect of GOES-18

For my first test using the Discovery Dish with the Az/El positioner, I decided to point at a relatively fixed satellite: GOES-18.

GOES-18 is a geostationary weather satellite, so from my location it appears at a nearly fixed azimuth and elevation. That makes it a good first target when validating the full receive chain.

For this setup, I am focused on L-band downlink collection (roughly the 1.69 GHz weather downlink region used by GOES products in many hobby workflows), then decoding and post-processing into viewable images.

Collection Setup

The first step was getting the hardware chain stable: dish, feed, low-noise receive path, SDR, and computer pipeline. I also wanted a deck-level view but this turned out to be less than ideal due to added thermal noise from the house.

Signal collection

After pointing and calibration, the next check is signal presence and quality at the SDR. At this stage I am looking for enough SNR margin for synchronization and decode. I used the open-source SatDump application for collection and processing.

Processed world views

Once signal was captured, various weather imagery products could be processed from the data and output for viewing. These include full-world style views and enhanced products useful for weather context.

There are more products and examples to show, but I will stop here. I started working on my own GOES-18 signal processing and decoding software, so I will add details about that to the post later. In addition, I plan to share more information about the antenna and positioner setup.
